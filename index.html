First principles

1. Specificity

The body adapts to repeated stress. Hampta is uphill stepping, downhill braking, long time on feet, and wet terrain. Stairs and long walks beat random extra gym volume.

2. Aerobic base

Easy run/walk makes breathing calmer by improving oxygen delivery and oxygen use. Trekking is hours of low to moderate output.

3. Eccentric braking

Downhill hurts because quads brake bodyweight while lengthening. Slow stair descents and controlled step-ups teach the pattern.

4. Strength to durability

Your gym base is raw material. Step-ups, lunges, calves, tibialis, and core convert it into one-leg-at-a-time mountain durability.

5. Recovery

Fitness appears after stress is absorbed. Sleep, protein, one rest day, and no failure leg days are the conversion process.

6. Taper

Last hard push is around July 11-12. Final week lowers fatigue so you arrive fresh, not trained but tired.

Diet defaults

Target: 82-83 kg by trek if it happens naturally. No crash diet.

Protein: 140-160 g/day. Water: about 3 L/day plus electrolytes if sweating. Carbs: keep rice, roti, or banana around workouts.
